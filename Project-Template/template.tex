\section{Overview}

We fine-tuned DistilBERT-base-uncased to assign each recipe in a 6-class corpus to its chef. After removing 14 exact duplicates, we retained 2,985 unique recipes and produced an 80/20 stratified split. The best checkpoint (early stopping after ~5 epochs) reaches 92.13\% validation accuracy and 0.916 macro-F1, a gain of roughly 49 percentage points over the strongest TF-IDF baseline.

\section{Data Preparation}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Parsed list fields (tags, ingredients, steps) and concatenated five text sections in the order \textit{name $\rightarrow$ ingredients $\rightarrow$ tags $\rightarrow$ description $\rightarrow$ steps}. This protects short, identifying fields from truncation.
    \item Dropped duplicated concatenated texts prior to splitting to prevent leakage (class counts range from 365 to 801 after deduplication).
    \item Tokenised with DistilBERT's WordPiece tokenizer at max length 512. The deduplicated corpus has a median of 234 tokens and 95th percentile of 418, so truncation is rare.
\end{itemize}

\section{Model and Training}

\begin{itemize}
    \item DistilBERT encoder with dropout 0.2, a 768\,$\rightarrow$\,768 projection, ReLU activation, and a final linear layer producing six logits.
    \item Optimiser: AdamW with learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, weight decay 0.01, linear warmup across 6\% of steps, gradient clipping at 1.0.
    \item Chill-mode batches (8 for training, 16 for evaluation) on Apple MPS. Evaluation every 100 steps with patience two evaluations keeps the best macro-F1 checkpoint.
\end{itemize}

Key observations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Validation accuracy and macro-F1 peak between epochs~4 and 5; later checkpoints oscillate slightly but do not improve macro-F1.
    \item Prediction distribution on the blind test set stays within $\pm$2.3 percentage points of the training prior (Chef~1533 mildly over-predicted, Chef~3288 slightly under-predicted).
    \item Misclassifications mostly confuse chefs 1533 and 3288, whose recipes overlap on small appetisers versus make-ahead meals.
\end{itemize}

\section{Lessons and Next Steps}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Deduplicating texts before splitting was essential; earlier runs that skipped this step overstated validation accuracy.
    \item The straightforward DistilBERT head is sufficient once evaluation cadence and early stopping are tightened; longer schedules offer little extra gain.
    \item Future improvements could add non-textual cues (quantities, durations), explore cross-validation for more stable estimates, and analyse attention patterns to separate stylistic cues from obvious keywords.
\end{itemize}
